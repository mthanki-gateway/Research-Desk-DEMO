# SIGNAL FROM VEGA — a short film

**Written by M. Okonkwo · Second draft · 14 March 2025 · 11 pages**

## Logline

A night-shift radio astronomer receives a signal that repeats her own
heartbeat back to her, eleven minutes before it happens.

## Characters

**DR. ADAORA NWOSU** (38) — radio astronomer. Precise, sleep-deprived, argues
with equipment as though it can hear her. Has not left the observatory in
nine days.

**TOMAS REYES** (60s) — night security. Reads paperbacks at the desk. Knows
more about the building than anyone on the science staff.

**THE SIGNAL** — never seen. Heard as a soft arrhythmic pulse, close to but
never exactly a heartbeat.

## Scene 1 — INT. CONTROL ROOM — NIGHT

*A long room lit by monitors. Rain on the skylight. ADAORA sits inside a
horseshoe of equipment, one shoe off, headphones on one ear.*

A waveform crawls across the central display. She stops it. Rewinds four
seconds. Plays it again.

**ADAORA:** That is not a pulsar.

*She types. The waveform redraws, cleaner.*

**ADAORA (CONT'D):** That is not a pulsar, and it is not the compressor on
the roof, and it is not my own equipment talking to itself, because I
unplugged my own equipment at four o'clock specifically so it would stop
lying to me.

*She reaches for a mug. It is empty. She drinks from it anyway.*

## Scene 2 — INT. CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS

*TOMAS walks the corridor with a torch he does not need — the lights are on.
He stops at the control room door, looks through the glass, and knocks twice.*

**TOMAS:** You are still here.

**ADAORA:** I am always still here.

**TOMAS:** That is what I am saying to you.

*He comes in. Sets a paper bag on the console. She does not look at it.*

**TOMAS (CONT'D):** It is a sandwich. I am not asking you to eat it. I am
asking you to be in the same room as it.

## Scene 3 — INT. CONTROL ROOM — LATER

*The waveform again. ADAORA has drawn on the screen with a marker, which is
clearly not allowed. TOMAS reads over her shoulder.*

**TOMAS:** What is the line for?

**ADAORA:** The line is where I think it starts.

**TOMAS:** And the other line?

**ADAORA:** The other line is where it actually starts, which is eleven
minutes earlier, which is the problem.

*Beat.*

**TOMAS:** Eleven minutes earlier than what?

**ADAORA:** Than me.

## Scene 4 — INT. CONTROL ROOM — MOMENTS LATER

*She has a pulse oximeter clipped to her finger. The reading on the small
screen: 71. On the main display, the signal, marked with her marker line:
also 71, eleven minutes ago.*

**ADAORA:** Ask me to do something.

**TOMAS:** What?

**ADAORA:** Anything. Something that would change it.

**TOMAS:** Stand up.

*She stands. Her reading climbs: 74, 78, 81.*

*On the display, the recorded signal from eleven minutes ago: 74, 78, 81.*

*TOMAS sits down slowly in the chair she has vacated.*

**TOMAS:** Sit back down, please.

## Scene 5 — INT. CONTROL ROOM — NIGHT

*The rain has stopped. The room is quieter for it. ADAORA has filled a
whiteboard with three separate arguments, two of which are crossed out.*

**ADAORA:** It is not prediction. Prediction is a claim about the future.
This is a recording. Something recorded me before I did it.

**TOMAS:** Those are the same thing.

**ADAORA:** They are not remotely the same thing. One of them is physics and
one of them is bookkeeping.

**TOMAS:** Which is this?

*She looks at the board for a long moment.*

**ADAORA:** I have been awake for nine days, Tomas.

## Scene 6 — INT. CONTROL ROOM — DAWN

*Grey light. The signal has stopped. The display shows flat noise. ADAORA is
asleep in the chair, headphones around her neck.*

*TOMAS puts his coat over her. He looks at the flat line on the screen for a
while. Then he leans towards the microphone, which is live, and which nobody
has told him is live.*

**TOMAS:** *(quietly)* Whoever you are. She is going to want to talk to you
when she wakes up.

*He turns away. On the display, unseen by either of them, the flat line
develops a small, regular, patient pulse.*

*CUT TO BLACK.*

## Production notes

The eleven-minute offset should never be explained. Early readers asked for a
mechanism and every version that supplied one was worse: the moment the film
says how, it stops being about a woman who has not slept in nine days and
starts being about a machine.

The signal is diegetic throughout. There is no score until the final black,
and what plays over the credits should be the signal itself, slowed by a
factor of eleven.

Budget note: the control room is the whole film. Shoot nights at the decommissioned
tracking station at Kaniva — the equipment there is dead but photographs as
live, which is cheaper than dressing a set and more convincing than either.
