# TIDEWATER — song lyrics and sleeve notes

**An original collection. Words and music by J. Halloran, 2025.**
All eleven songs here are original compositions written for this record.

## About the record

Tidewater was written across two winters in a rented cottage on the Solway
Firth, where the sea goes out further than seems reasonable and comes back
faster than seems safe. Nine of the eleven songs are about that, whether or
not they mention water.

The arrangements are deliberately thin. There is one guitar, one voice, and
on four tracks a harmonium that was broken in a way we decided to keep.

## 1. Low Water Mark

Verse one:

> I counted out the shoreline in the hours you were gone
> and the sand gave up its shipwrecks one by one
> there's a country underneath here that the water only rents
> and it shows itself for nothing and it goes back just as dense

Chorus:

> So set me at the low water mark
> where the map has not been written yet
> I will hold my ground in the dark
> and I will not take the tide as a threat

Verse two:

> You said a thing is only yours between the going and the turn
> which is a clever way of saying there is nothing left to learn
> but I have walked out past the marker where the guidebooks all stop short
> and the ground out there is honest, which is more than I was taught

## 2. Harmonium

> There is a note this thing can't hold
> and I have grown to love the gap
> the bellows breathe, the reed goes cold
> and something honest fills the lack

> Play me the song with the hole in it
> Play me the one you can't quite reach
> I have had my fill of the polished hit
> Give me the crack that lets it breach

## 3. Nine Days

Written in one sitting, about insomnia, and not about anyone in particular
regardless of what my sister says.

> Nine days and the kettle knows my name
> Nine days and the window's gone to grey
> I have stopped expecting sleep to come the usual way
> and started leaving out a saucer just in case

> The radio is talking to the hallway
> The hallway is not answering it back
> and somewhere in the middle of the small hours
> I mistake my own heart for a knock

## 4. Solway Crossing

Traditional in structure, original in every line. Call and response; on the
record the response is sung by whoever was in the room.

> *Will you cross?* — I will cross
> *With the water coming in?* — With the water coming in
> *And the light going out?* — And the light going out
> *Then you'll cross alone* — Then I'll cross alone
> *Then you'll cross alone.* — Then I'll cross.

## 5. Inventory

A list song. Everything in it was actually in the cottage.

> One chair that leans
> One kettle, loud
> One map of somewhere else
> One coat that isn't mine
> One radio, one shelf
> Two windows facing weather
> No lock upon the door
> and a note from the last tenant saying
> *the tide comes further than you think, take care*

## 6. The Long Field

> They put a fence across the long field
> and the field did not agree
> it kept its shape beneath the wire
> the way that water keeps the sea

> You can draw a line through anything
> it does not make it two
> I have been divided several times
> and I am still entirely you

## 7. Marram

Marram grass holds dunes together. The song is about that and nothing else,
which is a lie, but it is the lie the song prefers.

> Root down through the shifting
> Root down through the give
> Nothing here is solid
> That is not a reason not to live

> Marram in the salt wind
> Marram in the sand
> holding down a country
> with a thousand thin green hands

## 8. Winter Tenancy

> The lease runs out in April
> the light comes back in March
> I will have gone before the good months
> which was always the arrangement

> Do not send the deposit
> keep the coat, it suits the hook
> tell the next one what the tide does
> tell them earlier than I took

## 9. Bell on the Bank

A warning-bell song. The bell is real; it is on the far bank and it rings
when the crossing closes.

> Ring, and I will not come
> Ring, and I am already walking
> the difference between a warning and a summons
> is entirely in the listening

## 10. Low Water Mark (reprise)

Same melody, one verse, sung unaccompanied.

> I counted out the shoreline and I found it where I left it
> and the country underneath it does not care that I came back
> there is a kind of steadiness in things that do not need you
> and I have made my peace with being the one who keeps the track

## 11. Going Out

Instrumental. Harmonium and the sound of the room.

## Credits

Written and performed by J. Halloran. Recorded in the front room, February
2025. The harmonium was bought for eleven pounds and is not repaired.

No lyrics on this record are adapted from any existing work. Where a structure
is traditional — the call and response on track four, the list on track five —
the words are original and the debt is to the form, not to a source.
