# Notes on Ancient Monuments

## The Great Structures of Giza
The three largest tombs on the Giza plateau were built as burial chambers for
pharaohs of the Fourth Dynasty. The largest, constructed for Khufu around 2560
BCE, originally stood 146.6 metres tall and held that record for nearly four
millennia. Limestone blocks were quarried locally and floated down the Nile
during the annual inundation.

## Mummification Practices
Embalmers removed the internal organs and placed them in canopic jars, then
desiccated the body with natron salt for forty days before wrapping it in linen.
The heart was left in place, as it was believed to be the seat of thought and
would be weighed against a feather in the afterlife.

## Norwegian Cod Fisheries
The Lofoten islands have supported seasonal fishing since at least the Viking
age. Stockfish, air-dried on wooden racks through the winter, became Norway's
principal export commodity by the fourteenth century and remains a protected
designation today.

## Roman Aqueduct Engineering
Gradient was the central problem: too steep and the channel eroded, too shallow
and the water stagnated. Surveyors used the chorobates, a levelling instrument
five metres long, to maintain a fall of roughly 34 centimetres per kilometre
across distances exceeding fifty kilometres.
