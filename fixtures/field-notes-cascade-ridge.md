# Cascade Ridge — field notes, seasons 3 and 4

**Observer: R. Lindqvist · Plot network CR-01 to CR-14 · Elevation 840–1,620 m**

## Purpose and method

These are working notes from a long-term vegetation and fauna survey on the
eastern aspect of Cascade Ridge. Fourteen permanent plots, each 20 by 20
metres, were established in season 1 and are revisited four times a year.

The method is unchanged since season 1, deliberately: a survey whose protocol
drifts produces a time series that cannot be read. Every change since
establishment is listed in the appendix, and there are two.

## Plot CR-03 — subalpine transition

The most interesting plot in the network, because it is changing fastest.

Season 3 recorded 41 vascular species. Season 4 recorded 38, but the drop is
not loss: three species present in season 3 were recorded as seedlings that
did not establish, and the same three appeared again as seedlings in season 4.

Tree line in this plot has moved upslope approximately 4 metres since
establishment. That figure should be treated cautiously — it is within the
error of the method, and two observers disagreed about the boundary in
season 4.

## Plot CR-07 — north-facing scree

Almost nothing grows here, and that is the value of it. CR-07 is the control
against which colonisation in the other plots is read.

Season 4 recorded the first vascular plant in the plot interior: a single
cushion of moss campion, 6 centimetres across, in the lee of a boulder at the
north-east corner. It was photographed, measured and left alone.

If it survives a winter it is the most significant observation in four years
of this survey.

## Plot CR-11 — mature spruce

Closed canopy, low understorey diversity, high structural complexity. Species
count is stable at 19 to 22 across all four seasons.

Two standing dead stems fell between seasons 3 and 4, opening a gap of roughly
30 square metres. The understorey response is the thing to watch: gap dynamics
here are the mechanism by which this forest regenerates, and a gap has never
before formed inside a monitored plot.

Deadwood volume in this plot is now 41 cubic metres per hectare, high for the
region and consistent with a stand that has not been logged.

## Plot CR-14 — riparian corridor

Wettest plot in the network and the only one with standing water in three
seasons out of four.

Species count is the highest recorded anywhere on the ridge at 63, of which
roughly a third are recorded in no other plot. The corridor is doing more for
site-level diversity than its area suggests, which is an argument for
extending the network downstream rather than upslope.

Bank erosion at the northern edge has taken approximately 40 centimetres since
season 2. At that rate the plot boundary marker is threatened within a decade,
and moving it would break the time series. A decision is needed before it
becomes urgent rather than after.

## Fauna — incidental records

The survey is vegetation-focused; fauna records are incidental and should not
be read as systematic.

**Ptarmigan** — present in CR-03 and CR-07 in all seasons. A brood of six
recorded in CR-03, season 4, late July.

**Pine marten** — scat recorded in CR-11 and CR-12, seasons 3 and 4. No direct
observation in four years.

**Mountain hare** — tracks in snow, seasons 3 and 4, plots CR-01 to CR-05.
Numbers appear stable. That is an impression, not a measurement, and this
survey design cannot support a population claim.

**Golden eagle** — one bird, CR-07, season 4, hunting the scree. First record
for the network.

## Phenology

First flowering dates in plot CR-03, compared across seasons:

| Species         | Season 3 | Season 4 | Shift   |
| --------------- | -------- | -------- | ------- |
| Moss campion    | 8 June   | 2 June   | 6 days earlier |
| Alpine bistort  | 21 June  | 19 June  | 2 days earlier |
| Dwarf willow    | 14 June  | 11 June  | 3 days earlier |
| Trailing azalea | 5 June   | 5 June   | none    |

Two seasons is not a trend. It is recorded here so that it can become one, or
fail to, by season 8.

## Snowpack

The snow-free date at the CR-03 marker was 28 May in season 3 and 21 May in
season 4. The 2-metre snow stake was buried on 14 October in season 3 and 2
November in season 4.

A shorter snow season and an earlier melt is the most plausible driver of the
phenology shifts above, and it is worth saying plainly that this survey cannot
demonstrate that. It can only record both and let a longer series decide.

## Method changes since establishment

1. **Season 2:** photo points moved from the plot centre to the north-west
   corner, because the centre marker had become obscured by vegetation. All
   photographs from before season 2 are therefore not directly comparable.
2. **Season 4:** bryophyte identification moved from the field to the
   laboratory, after an audit found a 30 per cent misidentification rate in
   field records. Bryophyte counts before season 4 are indicative only.

## What the next observer should know

Get to CR-07 early in the day. The scree is stable in the morning and moves
underfoot by afternoon, once the ice within it has gone.

The upslope boundary of CR-03 is genuinely ambiguous and always has been. Two
observers will place it several metres apart. Use the photographs rather than
your judgement, and record which photograph you used.

Do not clear vegetation from the markers. The regrowth around them is data.

The river crossing below CR-14 is passable in the morning and frequently not
by late afternoon in the melt season. Plan the day so that plot is first.
